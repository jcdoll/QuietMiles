QUIET MILES — adjustable camera and surface study (revision 3)
Original PICO-8 code, art and sound inspired by the relaxed rally brief.

PLAY
1. Open https://www.pico-8-edu.com/ and click Play if needed.
2. Stop any running cartridge with Escape.
3. Type LOAD and press Enter; choose quiet_miles.p8.
4. Press Ctrl+R. Choose Drive with Z, or close settings with X.
The same .p8 file works in desktop PICO-8.

CONTROLS
Left / Right    Steer relative to the car
Z or Up         Accelerate
Down            Brake; hold at low speed to reverse
X               Handbrake (can be combined with Z)
P or Enter      PICO-8 pause menu

Pause-menu actions: drive settings, return to road, restart drive.
Escape in the development environment stops the cart; Ctrl+R restarts it.

SETTINGS
Up / Down       Select a row
Left / Right/Z  Cycle the selected setting
X               Return to driving

Road: Mixed alternates dirt and pavement around the main loop. The small
lookout spur remains dirt. Dirt/Pavement let you compare the same main
road without changing its geometry. Snow Test applies very low grip to
the whole region; it is an early surface test, not an authored snow level.

Hills: Rolling / Flat. Changing this rebuilds terrain and returns the car
to the road, so comparison starts from a sensible position.
View: Rally Low is the lower perspective; High View gives
a comparison camera. Both follow travel direction with delayed yaw.
Zoom: 100% to 150% in 10% steps. The default is now 120%; 100% reproduces
the previous wide framing. Selecting Zoom opens a preview: Left zooms
out, Right zooms in, Z cycles, X resumes driving. Up/Down returns to the
other settings. The car stays near the same screen height as you adjust.
Zoom works independently of View and keeps its value when changing
regions or restarting a drive. Ctrl+R resets the cartridge to 120%.
Feel: Rally / Assisted. Assisted increases rear grip to limit oversteer.
Place: Pine Lake / Ochre Hills. Both now have real terrain elevation.
Sound: a quiet original sketch; switch it off for a silent drive.

SUGGESTED COMPARISON
1. Use Rally Low, Zoom 120%, Rally feel, Rolling hills and Mixed road.
2. Accelerate into a bend, ease off, and use short steering inputs.
3. Briefly hold X while turning, then release and countersteer.
4. Compare Dirt and Pavement on the same bend. Pavement should gather
   the car sooner; dirt should carry a slide and need more room to stop.
5. Try Flat and High View independently to judge visibility and motion.
6. Snow Test should need earlier braking and smaller steering inputs.

WHAT CHANGED
Perspective projection replaces the old orthographic view. The low
camera points down about 27 degrees, sits 90 world units behind the
tracked position and 60 above the smoothed ground height. It follows
velocity more than body heading, keeping slides visible. No shake or
speed zoom. Rolling heights affect the road, scenery and car body pitch;
gravity changes uphill/downhill acceleration.
Manual zoom changes the lens magnification without changing camera
pitch or driving physics. Closer framing makes the car larger while
showing less of the surroundings. Camera settings are not saved to disk.

Separate front/rear lateral tyre forces, angular inertia, surface grip
limits, braking strength and rolling drag replace the old grip blend.
Grip changes are eased across surface boundaries.

SCOPE AND LIMITS
This revision deliberately focuses on free driving, camera and handling.
The earlier stage experiment is parked while those are tuned. Two small
finite regions; no connected world, infinite generation, jumps, damage,
saved progress or finished soundtrack. Snow uses the same roads and
scenery as a handling test. Terrain is a coarse mesh; road surfaces and
objects use simple layering rather than a general 3D depth buffer.

The cartridge presents at 30 fps with two 1/60-second physics steps per
frame. This fits the hilly perspective within PICO-8's VM budget without
switching between 60 and 30 fps during ordinary driving. Rendering at
60 fps would need further simplification. Region setup can briefly pause.

USEFUL FEEDBACK
Is the lower camera low/far enough while still showing the next bend?
Does the camera settle naturally after countersteering?
Is dirt playful and recoverable, or too reluctant to turn / too spinny?
Are changes in pavement and dirt grip clear without feeling abrupt?
